RootCause

The visual and interaction system for a tool that shows a teacher what their class actually thinks — not just what they got wrong.

Prometheus August AI Challenge — v1
01

Design principles

Four rules specific to this product, not general UX platitudes — each one exists because of something particular about diagnosing a classroom.

01

Diagnose, don't grade

No red ink, no scores on individual answers, nothing that reads as marking. The interface only ever shows patterns, never verdicts on a single student.

02

The teacher already knows their kids

Show what the data reveals and let the teacher connect names to patterns themselves. The tool surfaces structure; it doesn't narrate conclusions about who's struggling.

03

Calm enough to read between classes

Scannable in the two minutes before the next period starts. Quiet surfaces, one bold visual moment, no dense analytics console.

04

Real analysis, shown as real analysis

Every loading state names an actual pipeline stage. Never a generic spinner or a vague "processing" — the interface earns trust by being specific.

02

Visual system

Grounded in the product's own name: roots, growth, and what's underneath the surface of an answer. Warm and mineral rather than clinical, with one precise, data-driven accent reserved for the numbers themselves.

03

Information architecture & flow

One primary path. No settings menu, no nested navigation — a teacher moves forward through a single diagnostic, then either drills in or starts another.

New diagnostic→ Processing→ Dashboard→ Cluster detail
04

Screens

Six states covering the full MVP path, plus the two states that matter most for a demo audience: nothing yet, and not enough yet.

Screen 01 — New diagnostic

What did your class just answer?

Paste responses to one open-ended question. Works best with 10 or more.

One response per line — 14 responses added.

Asks for the correct concept up front — the teacher already knows the target, so the model isn't guessing at grading logic. "Question," not "prompt."

Misconception

Mass, not density

15 of 30 students

"Ice is heavy, but it's cold so it floats."

Suggested noteYou're right that temperature matters here — but it's density, not weight, that decides floating. Ice takes up more space than the same mass of liquid water, which is what keeps it up.

"It must be lighter than the water around it because it's frozen."

Suggested noteGood instinct that something about "frozen" matters. It's not lightness though — it's that freezing makes water take up more space, lowering its density.

Drilling into a cluster keeps the teacher in control of who gets what — the tool only ever drafts, it never sends anything on its own.

States exactly what happened and exactly what closes the gap — no vague "something went wrong."

05

Responsive & accessibility

  • Stacking: stat tiles and cluster cards collapse to a single column below 640px; the branch chart's labels are the first thing to simplify — under narrow widths it drops to a plain ranked list of shares instead of curved branches.
  • Never color-only: every badge carries a text label (Misconception / Unclear / Solid understanding), so meaning survives for colorblind users and in grayscale printouts.
  • Focus states: every interactive element gets a visible 2px focus ring in Well — see any button or link in this document via keyboard tab.
  • Live regions: the processing screen's stage list updates through an aria-live="polite" region so screen readers announce progress without needing to be re-focused.
  • Chart alternative: the branch diagram carries a full text summary in its aria-label rather than relying on the visual shape alone.
  • Contrast: body text targets WCAG AA against both Paper and Surface; muted text is reserved for secondary captions only, never for anything a user must act on.
